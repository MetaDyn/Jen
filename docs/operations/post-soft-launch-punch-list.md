# MetaDyn Post-Soft-Launch Punch List

## Purpose

This document is the practical operator checklist for the period immediately after MetaDyn's soft launch. The goal is not to do everything at once. The goal is to:

- monitor real demand without overreacting to silence
- stay ready for the first meaningful signup or reply
- tighten the onboarding path using real friction points
- preserve delivery throttle so early onboarding stays intentional
- convert early interest into proof, testimonials, and repeatable process

## Current Launch Context

As of this launch phase:

- initial launch communications have been sent
- social mentions are live
- managed onboarding is open now
- self-service deployment is targeted within ~30 days
- delivery is naturally throttled because onboarding does not begin until someone signs up

That throttle is strategically useful. It gives MetaDyn room to learn from the first few users before widening the funnel.

## Operating Principle

For this phase, optimize for:

1. clarity
2. responsiveness
3. low-friction onboarding
4. real-world learning
5. controlled expansion

Do **not** optimize for scale theater before the first few successful activations.

## Phase 1: Immediate Post-Launch Monitoring

### 1. Funnel Watch

Track the first signals coming in from the launch.

#### What to watch

- visits to `https://dashboard.metadyn.xyz/membership`
- completed membership signups
- inbound DMs, emails, and social replies
- questions about pricing, migration, self-hosting, or timing
- drop-off points during signup or checkout

#### What to log

For each meaningful inbound signal, capture:

- date/time
- source channel
- person or organization
- intent level
- what they asked
- what blocked them, if anything
- owner for follow-up
- next action

#### Success signal

A soft launch is working if it produces:

- qualified conversations
- at least a few serious expressions of interest
- at least one completed signup
- actionable friction data

No immediate replies does **not** mean failure.

### 2. Response Readiness

Be ready to respond quickly once interest appears.

#### Target

- same-day reply for serious inbound interest
- near-immediate acknowledgement when practical

#### Minimum reply goals

Each first response should:

- confirm receipt
- reduce uncertainty
- explain the next step
- avoid overwhelming the lead with too much process detail

## Phase 2: First-Contact And Onboarding Readiness

### 3. First-Response Flow

Prepare the exact flow for the first person who signs up or reaches out.

#### Minimum first-contact sequence

1. acknowledge signup or inquiry
2. thank them for joining early
3. explain what happens next
4. ask for the minimum required intake information
5. offer a scheduling path if a live conversation is needed

#### Recommended intake fields

- name
- organization
- contact email
- current space/platform status
- whether they are migrating from Spatial
- target use case
- desired timeline
- whether they want managed deployment or are waiting for self-service
- whether expedited migration/deployment is relevant

#### Owner questions

Define internally:

- who replies first
- who handles technical qualification
- who handles pricing or enterprise questions
- who schedules onboarding
- who owns the account once active

### 4. Signup Confirmation Experience

Pressure-test what a new customer sees immediately after signing up.

#### Verify

- payment/membership flow works end to end
- account state updates correctly after purchase
- the user is not left in a dead end
- confirmation messaging is clear
- the user knows whether onboarding is automatic or manual

#### Fix first

If there is any ambiguity after signup, fix that before trying to increase traffic.

### 5. Manual Onboarding SOP

Until self-service is fully live, make the manual path explicit.

#### Define the manual SOP

- trigger: what counts as a real onboarding event
- acknowledgement template
- intake collection method
- internal review step
- technical kickoff step
- delivery timeline expectation
- escalation path if onboarding hits blockers

#### Keep it lightweight

Do not build a giant process prematurely. Create the minimum repeatable path that can support the first 3-10 onboardings cleanly.

## Phase 3: Friction Reduction

### 6. Membership And Checkout QA

Run the full purchase and post-purchase path like a real customer.

#### Check for

- unclear pricing copy
- plan confusion between Individual vs Organization vs Enterprise
- failed or confusing checkout states
- broken redirects
- missing post-purchase instructions
- unclear onboarding expectations

#### Output

Create a short bug list with severity:

- P0: blocks purchase or blocks onboarding
- P1: causes confusion or reduces trust
- P2: polish issue, typo, or minor UX roughness

### 7. Documentation Minimum Viable Set

Do not wait for a full academy. Publish the minimum docs needed to reduce friction.

#### Minimum doc set

- what MetaDyn is for
- who each membership tier is for
- what happens after signup
- basic onboarding steps
- migration path for former Spatial users
- how managed onboarding differs from upcoming self-service
- how to contact MetaDyn for help

#### Priority rule

A short, clear doc that answers the top 5 questions is more valuable right now than a perfect long-form manual.

### 8. FAQ / Objection Handling

Prepare clean answers to the first likely objections.

#### Likely objections

- Why pay now if self-service is not fully live yet?
- What does managed onboarding include?
- What happens if there are issues during launch?
- How does this compare to Spatial's old model?
- What does “greater control” actually mean?
- What is the self-hosting/open-source path?
- Why is expedited migration/deployment a separate fee?
- Is MetaDyn appropriate for individuals, organizations, or enterprise only?

#### Goal

Answer these consistently, without sounding defensive.

## Phase 4: Controlled Demand Shaping

### 9. Early-Partner Strategy

Be intentional about which organizations come in first.

#### Ideal early partners

Prefer organizations that are:

- serious enough to complete onboarding
- collaborative and patient
- capable of giving useful feedback
- good future case-study candidates
- aligned with MetaDyn's ecosystem values

#### Decide now

Define:

- how many early partners MetaDyn wants at one time
- what “early-partner benefits” actually means
- what level of white-glove support is sustainable
- what kind of partner is not yet a fit

### 10. Throttle Rules

The launch should stay controllable.

#### Set explicit throttle rules

Examples:

- max concurrent managed onboardings
- max enterprise evaluations in parallel
- response-time targets by lead type
- criteria for waitlist mode
- criteria for widening promotion

#### Suggested practical thresholds

Start with internal rules like:

- `green`: 0-2 active onboardings, continue promotion
- `yellow`: 3-5 active onboardings, qualify tightly and pace starts
- `red`: 6+ active onboardings or repeated delivery slippage, shift to waitlist / staged intake

Tune these thresholds based on actual team capacity.

### 11. Follow-Up Communication Cadence

Not everyone who saw the launch message will respond immediately.

#### Plan a follow-up sequence

- short reminder to warm contacts
- public proof post once there is traction
- updated post when self-service is ready
- targeted outreach to strong-fit organizations

#### Important rule

Do not spam. Follow up when there is:

- a reason
- a proof point
- a clearer next step
- a narrower audience fit

## Phase 5: Proof, Learning, And Iteration

### 12. Proof Collection

The first proof points matter disproportionately.

#### Capture as soon as possible

- first paid signup
- first successful onboarding
- first migrated space
- first organization live on MetaDyn
- first testimonial or quote
- first measurable outcome or success story

#### Why it matters

Proof reduces future sales friction more than additional abstract positioning ever will.

### 13. Post-Inbound Debrief Loop

After every meaningful lead conversation, ask:

- what did they understand immediately?
- what confused them?
- what made them hesitate?
- what made them lean in?
- what should be clarified in product, pricing, docs, or messaging?

Use early interactions to refine the path, not just close the lead.

### 14. Internal Learning Log

Keep a simple running log of:

- objections heard
- recurring questions
- onboarding blockers
- implementation surprises
- feature gaps exposed by real users
- sales copy that resonates
- sales copy that confuses

This becomes the source material for:

- product fixes
- FAQ updates
- onboarding improvements
- future launch messaging

## Operator Checklist

Use this as the active punch list.

### Today / Immediate

- [ ] Confirm launch messages and social posts are all live
- [ ] Confirm membership page is functioning
- [ ] Confirm signup-to-account-state path works
- [ ] Define who owns first-response handling
- [ ] Draft first-response template
- [ ] Draft onboarding intake template
- [ ] Draft FAQ for top objections
- [ ] Decide practical onboarding throttle threshold
- [ ] Set up a simple lead / signup tracking log

### Next 24-72 Hours

- [ ] Monitor for replies, DMs, signups, and drop-off signals
- [ ] Run a real end-to-end membership flow test
- [ ] Patch any confusing post-signup dead ends
- [ ] Publish or refine minimum viable onboarding docs
- [ ] Define early-partner criteria
- [ ] Define what counts as a qualified lead vs casual interest
- [ ] Prepare reminder/follow-up message for warm leads if needed

### After First Signup

- [ ] Send acknowledgement quickly
- [ ] Capture intake information
- [ ] Confirm owner and next step internally
- [ ] Move them into managed onboarding flow
- [ ] Record friction points encountered
- [ ] Ask for feedback at the appropriate time
- [ ] Capture proof/testimonial opportunity if experience is positive

### After First 3-5 Onboardings

- [ ] Review where users hesitated most
- [ ] Refine docs and FAQ based on real questions
- [ ] Refine pricing/page copy based on misunderstandings
- [ ] Reassess staffing and throttle rules
- [ ] Decide whether to widen promotion or keep pacing controlled

> Thanks for signing up and joining MetaDyn early. We’re excited to help get your space online. We’ll follow up with the next onboarding step shortly, including the info we need to get your deployment moving.

### First Intake Prompt

> To get started, could you send over your organization/project name, your primary use case, whether you’re migrating from Spatial, your preferred timeline, and whether you’re looking for managed onboarding now or planning to use self-service once it opens?

### Warm Follow-Up Message

> Just following up on the MetaDyn launch note in case the timing is good now. We’re currently onboarding a limited number of builders and organizations directly, and I’d be happy to help you figure out whether managed onboarding or the upcoming self-service path is the better fit.

## Metrics That Actually Matter In This Phase

Ignore vanity metrics unless they correlate with real progress.

Prioritize:

- qualified replies
- completed signups
- onboarding starts
- successful activations
- speed from signup to human follow-up
- recurring friction themes
- proof points earned

Treat these as secondary for now:

- raw impressions
- likes without intent
- generic positive comments without follow-through

---

## Decision Gates

### Gate 1: Is the market signal real enough to keep pushing?

Indicators:

- serious replies
- signups
- repeated fit-aligned questions
- at least some willingness to onboard during managed phase

### Gate 2: Is the onboarding path clean enough to increase promotion?

Indicators:

- users understand what happens after signup
- payment path is working cleanly
- manual onboarding load is manageable
- the team can respond quickly enough

### Gate 3: Is there enough proof to strengthen broader promotion?

Indicators:

- first live success
- first testimonial
- first migration story
- first repeatable onboarding pattern

---

## Strategic Reminder

This phase is not about proving that MetaDyn can make noise.

It is about proving that MetaDyn can:

- attract the right builders
- move them into onboarding
- deliver successfully
- learn fast
- expand from a stronger base

A quiet first day is not a problem.
A broken first onboarding is a problem.

Optimize accordingly.
