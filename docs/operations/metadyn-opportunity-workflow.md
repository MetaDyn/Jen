# MetaDyn Opportunity Workflow

_Last updated: 2026-05-18_

This is the lightweight operating workflow for moving opportunities through MetaDyn CRM without overcomplicating the pipeline.

## Purpose

The goal is not to create perfect sales bureaucracy.
The goal is to make sure opportunities do not float.

Every active opportunity should answer five questions clearly:

1. **What is it?**
2. **Why do we think it matters?**
3. **Who owns it?**
4. **What happens next?**
5. **When do we check again?**

## Core operating fields

For every meaningful opportunity, keep these current:

- **Stage**
- **Owner**
- **Current thesis**
- **Next action**
- **Next checkpoint**

If those five are current, the opportunity is usable.
If they are stale, the opportunity is drifting.

## Stage definitions

### SCREENING
Use when:
- the opportunity has been identified
- research has been done
- the angle is being shaped
- likely contacts or funding routes have been identified
- outreach may be drafted but meaningful engagement has not happened yet

Required at this stage:
- company/opportunity exists in CRM
- at least one strong summary note exists
- likely funding route or buyer logic is defined
- likely contact route is defined
- owner is assigned
- next action is explicit

Typical examples:
- researched museum / civic / brand opportunity
- concept has been framed
- outreach target identified
- first email not yet sent, or no reply yet

### MEETING
Use when:
- outreach has produced a real conversation
- a meeting is scheduled or completed
- there is active two-way engagement beyond cold research

Required at this stage:
- note with who replied / who attended
- note with what they cared about
- explicit next step after the meeting

### PROPOSAL
Use when:
- a scoped concept, pilot, budget frame, or structured ask is in play
- the opportunity is no longer abstract and is being shaped into a real offer

Required at this stage:
- current solution framing
- rough scope or phases
- likely budget or funding path
- decision path / blocker notes

### CUSTOMER
Use when:
- the opportunity has converted into an active client relationship, funded engagement, or live deal in execution

Required at this stage:
- confirmation of what was agreed
- internal owner clarity
- delivery / follow-through visibility

## Opportunity movement rules

### 1. Create the opportunity early
Create the opportunity once it is real enough to track.
Do not wait for perfect certainty.

### 2. Add one strong synthesis note
Do not scatter context across ten tiny notes if one clean summary note would do.
A good synthesis note should capture:
- strategic fit
- likely funding path
- likely buyer/contact route
- key risks
- recommended next move

### 3. Assign an owner quickly
If an opportunity matters, it needs one named operator.
No floating ownership.

Owner means:
- they are the person driving the next motion
- they are not necessarily the final closer
- they are responsible for keeping the record aligned with reality

### 4. Every note should help answer one of three things
A useful note should clarify at least one of:
- **what we learned**
- **what we decided**
- **what happens next**

### 5. Use stage changes sparingly
Do not advance stage because work happened internally.
Advance stage because the opportunity itself moved.

Examples:
- research completed -> usually still SCREENING
- outreach drafted -> still SCREENING
- outreach sent but no reply -> still SCREENING unless team wants a separate outreach stage later
- reply received / conversation started -> MEETING
- concept / pilot / scope / budget path being discussed -> PROPOSAL
- funded / active execution -> CUSTOMER

## Owner workflow

The owner should keep the following current:

- last meaningful action
- next meaningful action
- current external contact route
- current internal thesis
- blockers / waiting state

If nothing has happened for too long, the owner should either:
- push the next motion
- reclassify the opportunity
- or explicitly park it

## Recommended note pattern

For active opportunities, prefer this sequence:

1. **Initial strategy note**
   - why it fits
   - strongest angle
   - early risk / route

2. **Contact-path note**
   - who to reach
   - why them
   - likely funding route

3. **Action/status note**
   - outreach drafted
   - outreach sent
   - reply received
   - meeting scheduled
   - internal reassignment

4. **Meeting / proposal note**
   - what was discussed
   - what matters to them
   - next commitment

## Practical rules for alignment

### For Josh
Josh should be able to open any active opportunity and quickly see:
- the thesis
- the owner
- the next move
- whether this is real or stale

### For Chris or another operator
The operator should not need to reread the full history every time.
The record should make the next step obvious.

### For Jen
Jen should help keep opportunities structured by:
- assigning ownership when asked
- writing synthesis notes instead of fragmented chat residue
- logging meaningful status changes
- not over-advancing stages without an external signal

## Current default operating interpretation

Right now, MetaDyn should treat:
- **SCREENING** as research + shaping + pre-engagement
- **MEETING** as live two-way engagement
- **PROPOSAL** as scoped ask / pilot / budget path in motion
- **CUSTOMER** as live/funded relationship

## Near-term improvement ideas

As CRM usage matures, add:
- explicit follow-up tasks with assignees
- standard note templates for outreach / meeting / proposal
- owner dashboards for active opportunities
- a parked / dormant convention if needed
- a dedicated outreach stage later only if it proves useful
