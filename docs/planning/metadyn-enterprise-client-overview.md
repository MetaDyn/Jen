# MetaDyn
## Immersive Enterprise Experiences. Contextual AI. Greater Control.

MetaDyn brings people and AI together in interactive, browser-accessible environments built around real-world places, equipment, and processes. We help organizations explore how immersive technology can improve training, collaboration, and understanding—while treating deployment control and security requirements as part of the conversation from the start.

## What Makes Our Platform Different

**Built for more than virtual meetings.** MetaDyn's Unity-based platform supports rich, interactive environments where teams can explore a facility, examine equipment, or rehearse a procedure together. The experience is organized around the work, not just the room.

**AI that participates in the experience.** Voice-enabled, embodied AI provides a foundation for contextual guides and assistants within the environment. Instead of moving between a 3D model, a document, and a separate chatbot, users can ask questions while exploring the subject at hand.

**More control over deployment and continuity.** MetaDyn supports self-hosting of immersive experiences. Combined with our open SDK strategy, this gives organizations a path toward greater customization and less dependence on a single hosted destination. Deployment options can be evaluated against the organization's infrastructure and operating requirements.

## A More Controlled Approach to Enterprise Security

For an enterprise, the important question is not simply whether an experience looks compelling. It is whether the organization can understand and control how that experience handles access, information, and AI interaction.

MetaDyn's approach focuses on four practical areas:

- **Deployment boundaries:** Self-hosting can place delivery of the immersive experience on infrastructure the organization controls. We distinguish that experience layer from connected identity, voice, multiplayer, and AI services so customers can assess the complete data path.
- **Controlled information access:** The deployment scope should define who can enter, what information they can access, and which functions require additional privileges. Required controls must be validated before sensitive use.
- **Bounded AI interaction:** AI knowledge sources and permitted actions should be explicit. An assistant that explains a procedure should not automatically have permission to change an operational system.
- **Low-risk evaluation:** Start with non-sensitive content, a limited audience, and a clearly defined task. Review security and integration requirements before introducing sensitive information or production connections.

**Our security value is greater deployment choice and explicit boundaries—not a blanket claim that self-hosting or immersive technology is inherently secure.** Specific controls, external dependencies, and operating responsibilities are assessed for each proposed deployment.

## Applying This to Digital Twins

Immersive representations of facilities, assets, and processes can give teams a shared reference for training, planning, and security preparedness. Contextual AI can help users navigate information and understand procedures within that representation.

Initial applications could include:

- Guided employee onboarding and procedural training.
- Collaborative exploration of facilities and complex assets.
- Rehearsal of security scenarios, escalation procedures, and incident coordination without affecting live operations.

Where a project requires a connected digital twin, live data sources, update frequency, and access boundaries become explicit integration requirements. A training model or simulation is kept distinct from verified live operational state.

## What You Can Expect From MetaDyn

A clear discussion of what the platform supports today, which services a proposed experience depends on, where information would flow, and what additional work your requirements call for. We work toward an agreed scope rather than asking customers to rely on broad security assurances.

**A practical first step:** select one enterprise workflow and evaluate it using non-sensitive content, agreed deployment boundaries, and measurable success criteria—with business, technology, and security stakeholders involved from the outset.

*Security capabilities and suitability are deployment-specific. This overview does not imply security certification, regulatory approval, or fully private operation of all connected services.*
